## Design Context

### Users

Players who want a short, approachable session to read a market signal, make a trade, and understand its outcome. The experience is a fictional investment game, not financial advice.

### Brand Personality

Playful, legible, and deliberate. It should feel like a friendly retro market game while keeping financial values trustworthy and easy to scan.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a bright pixel inspired visual language: crisp edges, a restrained palette, and small pixel motifs for character. Pair it with the dense, legible retro trading terminal atmosphere of STONKS-9800: compact market data, company lists, charts, and clear trade feedback. Interpret these references in original UI; do not copy their assets, branding, or exact screens.

Use bright blue for primary actions and warm yellow for rewards or selected highlights. Preserve the existing semantic meaning of rising and falling market colors and pair color with labels, arrows, or values. Prefer flat surfaces, clear borders, and compact spacing over glass effects, decorative gradients, or excessive rounding.

### Design Principles

1. The loop is briefing → compare → trade → market reaction → reflect. Make the next useful action obvious at each step.
2. Cash, asset value, price, change rate, holdings, and trade quantity must be easy to distinguish at a glance.
3. Pixel styling must not impair Korean text, numeric alignment, contrast, or touch targets.
4. On narrow screens, prioritize briefing, stock comparison, order controls, then summary. Keep essential trading information available without horizontal scrolling.
5. Use motion only to explain state changes or trade feedback, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.
6. Preserve existing game rules, routes, local save data, and ranking contracts during visual migration unless the user separately requests a behavior change.
